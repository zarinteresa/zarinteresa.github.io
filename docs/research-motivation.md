# Research motivation & problem statement (draft v0.1)

> Brainstorm, not a final decision. Evidence below is from web searches on 2026-10-20 (links at bottom); I read abstracts/snippets, not full papers — **read the papers before citing them in an SOP.**

## 1. Direction in one line
**Human-centered computing for women's everyday wellbeing** — using ordinary, low-stigma activities (cooking, plants, daily self-recording) as entry points for tools that fit women of different ages, classes, abilities and mental-health conditions. Method-wise: qualitative first (interviews, contextual inquiry, co-design), with light prototyping. This matches your interest in qualitative work and your record (teaching kids, Figma design, campus platform).

## 2. What the literature says (and where the gaps are)
| Theme | What exists | Gap you could fill |
|---|---|---|
| Mental-health apps | A 2025 meta-analysis of 92 RCTs finds apps help overall (g ≈ 0.43) and that persuasive design relates to engagement; most trial samples skew female, but it isn't women-specific. | Few studies design *for* women's differing contexts, rather than just recruiting them. |
| Women-specific design | Studies for Black American women (trust, ownership/data transparency, community) and **iCare** for working-class women in India (prototype, 40 women). | Little on South Asian women across class/ability; findings rarely transfer across cultures. |
| Bangladesh / HCI4D | Afreen et al. 2024 (rural women: stigma, low mental-health literacy, community participatory design); **dMENTHOL** (urban women, PHQ-9 screening); Sharifa Sultana's feminist HCI work on rural women under patriarchal constraints. | Mostly *screening/information*; few *everyday-life* tools, teens, or women with disabilities. |
| Accessible cooking | Strong work on cooking with **visual** impairments (Li et al., ASSETS 2021, 2024; CHI 2024). | My searches found little on **motor** disabilities, kids/teens, or cooking as wellbeing. (Search more: "adaptive kitchen", "upper-limb", "dexterity".) |
| Plants / gardening | A pilot RCT in women found gardening improved mood and reduced stress vs. art; Thrive's "Cultivating Wellbeing" app is evaluated (interim report 2025); digital therapeutic gardens associated with higher life satisfaction (cross-sectional, Korea). | No controlled evidence on a *beginner plant-care app* for women; mixed study sizes in reports. |
| Vlogs / self-recording | Teen vlogs as identity work; adolescent menstrual-pain vlogs; UWE co-designing vlogs with girls for body image. | Private-first, low-pressure video diaries for girls/women with mental-health needs are under-studied. |

## 3. Candidate problem statements (pick/merge)
**A. (broad, recommended)**
> Women's wellbeing technology is usually designed for one "typical" user and delivered as clinical screening or social-media-style sharing. This overlooks how women of different ages, incomes, physical abilities and mental-health conditions actually cope day to day — through ordinary routines like cooking, tending plants and recording their day. **How can everyday-activity technologies be co-designed with diverse women so they are accessible, low-stigma, and supportive of wellbeing — especially in resource-constrained settings like Bangladesh?**

**B. (narrower, easier to scope for a 1-year MS)**
> How do women with physical disabilities and teen girls experience cooking, and what design features make a cooking companion usable, safe and confidence-building?

**C. (mental-health focus)**
> What do women living with depression/anxiety in Dhaka want from a private, non-clinical digital space (plants, journaling), and how does stigma shape that?

**Possible research questions for A**
1. What everyday routines do women across age/class/ability already use for wellbeing, and what blocks them?
2. How should accessibility (motor, cognitive, literacy, cost, connectivity) be built into these tools?
3. How do privacy and family/community stigma shape whether women use such tools?

## 5. Cautions
- Mental health research involves vulnerable participants → ethics/IRB, trauma-informed methods, no diagnosis claims.
- Don't claim "easy": reviewers see through it. Frame as *human-centered, interdisciplinary*.
- Choose one population for the SOP (e.g. women with disabilities, or teen girls), mention others as future scope.
